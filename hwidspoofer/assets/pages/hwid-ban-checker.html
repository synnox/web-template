What is an HWID ban?

An HWID ban — short for Hardware ID ban — associates a device fingerprint with an enforcement record. Unlike an account ban, which follows one login, a hardware restriction can recognise the same PC across separate accounts. A game's anti-cheat can combine firmware, storage, Windows, network, and platform signals into that fingerprint; vendors do not publish every field or weight they use.

Major anti-cheat systems like EasyAntiCheat, BattlEye, RICOCHET, and Byfron all operate at a level where recurring machine signals matter. Windows reinstalls replace OS-resident values but normally leave firmware and physical-device values in place, which is why a reinstall alone cannot prove that a machine profile changed.

What are the four types of game bans?

The checker compares four restriction patterns: account, IP, hardware, and temporary. These categories have different scopes, so the same symptom does not establish the same ban type. For a side-by-side breakdown of the three that get confused most often, read HWID ban vs IP ban vs account ban.

1. Account ban

An account ban applies to a specific login or profile. If another authorised account works normally on the same PC, that result fits an account-scoped restriction better than a machine-wide block; it does not override the publisher's account or device-linking rules.

2. IP ban

An IP ban applies to a public network address. A different network changing the symptom supports an IP-based explanation, but a VPN result alone is not proof because routing, region, and server availability can change at the same time.

3. HWID ban (hardware ban)

An HWID ban associates recurring machine signals with enforcement. New accounts failing on the same PC while working elsewhere is a stronger hardware-level symptom than one account notice. A VPN or fresh Windows installation does not normally change motherboard, disk, and other firmware-resident values.

4. Temporary suspension

A temporary suspension has a stated or policy-defined expiry. Publishers set different durations and escalation rules, so the notice and official policy control the answer. For examples across games including Overwatch 2, Apex Legends, and Rainbow Six Siege, compare the stated length collected in the ban duration database, which reproduces the publisher's own wording instead of estimating a number.

What are the signs of an HWID ban?

The strongest HWID-ban signs repeat at the machine level: separate accounts fail on the same PC, a network change does not change the symptom, and reinstalling Windows leaves it in place. No single sign proves the publisher's private classification.

  • •New accounts get banned almost immediately. You create a fresh account with a new email, and within minutes or hours it's banned — even though you haven't done anything on it. Repeated machine-specific failure is a strong hardware-level symptom, subject to the publisher's account-linking rules.
  • •A VPN doesn't help. If changing networks has no effect, an IP-only explanation is less likely. The result does not identify hardware by itself.
  • •Reinstalling Windows didn't work. A clean Windows install resets the software environment but normally leaves firmware and physical-device values intact. The unchanged symptom therefore supports a persistent-machine explanation without proving one.
  • •Other people's accounts also get banned on your PC. If another authorised account works elsewhere but fails on your machine, the PC is the recurring variable. Account or household linking can still affect the result.
  • •The anti-cheat issued a specific ban notice. Some anti-cheats distinguish between account actions and hardware actions in their notifications. If the message references a "machine ban" or device restriction, treat that wording as stronger evidence than a generic "permanent ban," which can still be account-scoped.

Quieter restrictions muddy the picture. Matchmaking that silently pools you with other flagged players never shows a ban screen, and telling a shadow ban apart from a hardware ban takes a different set of checks. If the notice arrived in the last few hours, work through the first moves after getting banned before you change anything on the machine.

Multiple matching signs strengthen the hardware-ban pattern, but they do not turn the checker into an official publisher record. HWID ban checker FAQs

An HWID ban is a hardware-associated restriction that uses one or more device signals alongside account or network evidence. It can associate additional accounts seen on the same device, but it does not guarantee that every account will be banned; the publisher’s notice and appeal record define the known enforcement scope.

An account ban applies to a named account, while a hardware restriction associates enforcement with device signals and can affect additional accounts seen on that device. Publishers can combine account, device, IP, payment, or behavior signals, so neither label alone proves the full scope.

A Windows reinstall can regenerate OS-level values such as MachineGuid and volume metadata, but it does not replace firmware or device identities such as baseboard serials, drive firmware serials, physical NIC addresses, or TPM-rooted signals. A reinstall can fix client or cache problems; it is not proof that a hardware restriction has cleared.

What is ACE?

Anti-Cheat Expert (ACE) is Tencent's game-security platform. Its PC deployments protect Tencent-linked competitive titles such as Delta Force and Arena Breakout Infinite, while the wider ACE product also spans client hardening, server-side analysis, and other platforms. That Tencent portfolio is the page's defining context.

Tencent describes the PC service in terms of kernel-level inspection, client and server SDKs, automated analysis, and machine-based banning. Each game can ship a tailored module and policy, so an ACE label does not prove that Delta Force, Wuthering Waves, and Strinova use an identical field list or one universal ban decision.

How does ACE track hardware?

ACE's public PC material confirms machine-based banning and kernel-level inspection but does not disclose a complete identifier schema. On Windows, the concrete board and memory surfaces are `Win32_BaseBoard`, `Win32_PhysicalMemory`, and the SMBIOS Type 2 baseboard serial behind the WMI view. Storage can be read through `Win32_DiskDrive` or checked at the device stack with `IOCTL_STORAGE_QUERY_PROPERTY`.

A deployment can supplement those hardware values with `MachineGuid` under `HKLM\SOFTWARE\Microsoft\Cryptography`, Windows installation data, volume identifiers, and MAC enumeration. Kernel inspection makes cross-checking important: adapter metadata, WMI results, and controller responses can expose an inconsistent partial change even when one user-mode value looks new.

The TPM 2.0 endorsement key is available through the TBS API, while Secure Boot state reports boot-chain trust. Tencent's public ACE pages do not confirm that every listed PC game collects that key. Those surfaces should be discussed as inputs a tailored deployment can use, with the exact choice left to the game-specific module.

Machine-based banning implies a normalized relationship among multiple signals, not one magic serial. Composite or fuzzy matching can preserve confidence when a player replaces a drive or reinstalls Windows. The same raw identifiers may recur across Tencent ACE titles, creating re-flagging exposure, but enforcement remains dependent on the publisher, title, and ACE deployment.
